# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF
## What is strong
- **The pitch fits in one sentence, and it describes the experience.** "Votre salle pleine. Vos commandes en direct. Zéro commission." People will be using it during service ("pendant que vous, vous cuisinez"). That is the moment the product is designed for.
- **The pains are concrete, real moments.** The phone ringing mid-service, the table of 8 that does not show on a Saturday, the out-of-date menu. Deposits with automatic refunds, reminders the day before and 2 h before, and self-service cancelling answer the no-show moment from start to finish.
- **The first five minutes look like they were thought about.** Importing the menu from a photo, PDF or Excel, a website that is ready once the menu and hours are in, six guided steps and a test mode before going live. This starts from what the owner wants to do first and works back.
- **One plan, nothing optional.** The buyer has nothing to choose, so the pricing is simple.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** Seven modules (bookings, ordering and delivery, table service with a floor plan and a till, menu, website, reviews and loyalty, management) each go up against a specialist tool. Doing all seven brilliantly is the hard part. Doing seven at an average level means losing to the specialist on each one. How much of it is built and in production is unknown. If the table-service and payment side is shaky during a Saturday rush, the restaurant will drop the whole thing, bookings included.
2. **The product hands over to other systems at the riskiest points.** The restaurant connects its own payment account. Bancontact payments at the terminal are only "noted". The restaurant's own staff deliver. SMS, Google reviews and the Meta pixel run on third-party services. Each of these handovers is a point where a customer's payment, booking or order can fail, and the restaurant will blame this product. The setup is done for the restaurant only during the beta. Whether a paying restaurant connecting its own payment account gets through the six steps alone is not yet proven.
3. **Part of the experience does not exist yet.** The website is in French only. Whether Dutch- or English-speaking diners in Belgium can book is an open question, and so is whether the product can serve restaurants outside the French-speaking market. The site's examples are fictional, so no restaurant has yet been seen running a full service on it. Answer with a buyer panel and the beta results.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- If you could keep only one module, which one would it be, and why would a restaurant switch for that one alone?
- Walk me through a Saturday at 20:30: a walk-in, a takeaway order and a cancelled table for 8 arrive in the same minute. What does each screen show?
- Which modules are in production today with real restaurants, and which are still in progress?
- What happens when the payment provider, SMS or delivery fails mid-service, and who does the owner call?
- How long does a restaurant take to go live without done-for-you setup?
- What does each restaurant cost to serve (SMS, AI, support)? That is for the CFO's unit economics. Who already sells this all-in-one bundle in Belgium? That is for competitor research.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Name the one module the product leads with, and make it work brilliantly before marketing the rest. Bookings with deposits looks like the strongest candidate.
2. All 3 beta restaurants run real services on that module within the 30 days the beta allows. Record the first five minutes and the first Saturday for each, and publish those results instead of the fictional examples.
3. Map every handover (payments, SMS, delivery, reviews), say what the restaurant sees when each one fails, and test those failures before charging the first paying restaurant.
4. Show that a restaurant gets through the six-step setup without help before the done-for-you support stops.
5. Decide on the language question (French only, or Dutch too) before going after customers beyond the French-speaking market.
