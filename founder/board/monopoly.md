# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market that could be owned first.** Independent, French-speaking restaurants in Belgium is narrow enough to dominate. The beta limits who can apply to Belgium, and features like Bancontact and a French-language site fit that local market.
- **Zero commission is a structural position, not just a discount.** A flat fee "quel que soit votre volume" and payments going straight to the restaurant set it against delivery platforms in the one place they cannot follow without hurting their own model.
- **The bundle could create switching costs.** Bookings, ordering, table service, the website, customer cards with visit history and loyalty campaigns in one place mean that, over time, the restaurant's customer data and daily routine live inside the product. If that happens, leaving gets expensive.
- **A cooperative company (SComm)** might later become a brand or a trust story with independents. Today that is only a possibility.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proprietary edge in a crowded category.** Every module (bookings with deposits, QR menus, online ordering, a site builder, AI review replies) is a known feature type. The brief names no competitor except "plateformes de livraison" and "téléphone et carnet", but established booking, POS and ordering tools very likely reach the same buyer. Nothing shown is ten times better. Being all-in-one and commission-free is an incremental improvement that a well-funded competitor can copy by month six. Who these competitors are and what they charge is an open question for **competitor research**.
2. **Distribution is unproven and may not scale.** Customers come in through a call-back form, 30-minute calls and done-for-you setup. That is founder-led selling with no visible channel to the first 100 restaurants, and there are no network effects: diners do not bring in other restaurants. The cost of acquiring one restaurant is unknown (**CFO's unit economics**). Whether owners respond to "zéro commission" or are tied to platforms for discovery is a question for a **buyer panel**.
3. **Durability and the team are unknown.** It is not known how much of the product is built, who builds it, or the runway. The cost to serve SMS, AI, hosting and support for each restaurant is also unknown (**CFO**). A broad product run by an unknown team, at a lifetime price of 98.50 €, may not survive ten years or build economies of scale.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is your secret? What do you know about Belgian independents that booking, POS and delivery players do not?
- Which single segment (one city, one cuisine, one service type) will you own first, and what share of it can you realistically win?
- Why now? What has changed that makes a commission-free, all-in-one tool win today?
- How do the first 100 restaurants arrive, and what does each cost to sign and set up?
- What stops a booking or POS incumbent from adding commission-free ordering and a site builder in six months?
- Which of the seven modules is genuinely better than the best single-purpose tool, and by how much?
- Who is on the team, and how much of this is live in production today?
- Could anything create a network effect, for example diners carrying one account across restaurants or shared demand?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Competitor research maps the direct and indirect alternatives in Belgium, with prices, and names one defensible gap.
2. The founder names a beachhead (a city or segment) and a repeatable channel, with an estimated cost to acquire a restaurant that the CFO confirms is covered by its monthly margin.
3. The three beta restaurants go live within 30 days and show use that would be hard to leave: bookings or orders processed, and customer records built up.
4. The founder states, in one sentence, the advantage that will compound (data, brand, integration depth or local scale) and a 12-month plan to build it.
