# Offers lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The pain is real and named well.** The phone ringing mid-service, no-shows on a Saturday table of 8, and platform commissions are concrete, costly problems the buyer feels every week.
- **The buyers are easy to find.** Independent restaurants in Belgium are physically visible, listed on maps and reachable in person. Targeting is tight.
- **The dream outcome is strong:** "Votre salle pleine. Vos commandes en direct. Zéro commission."
- **The stack is wide.** Bookings with deposits, ordering, table service, menu import, website, reviews and loyalty all come in one plan. Each one solves a specific problem on the way to the outcome.
- **The scarcity is real if it is held:** -50 % for life for the first 20 restaurants, and 3 beta places. The beta asks for commitment in return (live in 30 days, weekly feedback), which is the right trade.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood: there is no proof.** The site uses "restaurant et chiffres fictifs", and the number of current customers is unknown. The 16 818 € figure is a calculator estimate, not a result. Without proof, a restaurant owner has no reason to believe the outcome will happen, and this is where most offers fail.
2. **Effort: the restaurant has to bring its own demand.** Delivery platforms bring customers, and this product does not. Zero commission saves nothing if direct orders never arrive. On top of that, the restaurant has to move bookings, ordering and payments to a new system and deliver with its own staff. That is a heavy ask of an owner who "has a little time". It is unknown how many diners would actually switch to ordering direct. A buyer panel would answer that.
3. **The pitch competes on price.** The headline difference is "no commission", and 197 € / month is set against platform fees. If competing booking, ordering and POS tools offer similar features, this becomes a commodity. Competitors are unknown, so competitor research is needed. Whether restaurant budgets and margins can carry 197 € / month, and what each restaurant costs to serve (SMS, AI, support), are open questions for the CFO's unit economics.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many restaurants are live today, and what measured result has any of them had (fewer no-shows, direct orders per week, commission saved)?
- What guarantee do you offer? For example, a refund if no-shows or commission savings do not reach a set level within 90 days.
- How fast does a restaurant see its first direct booking or order, and who does the setup work?
- What do you do to move the restaurant's existing platform customers to ordering direct?
- Could you charge 394 € / month and still be the obvious choice? If not, what is missing?
- Will the "first 20" limit really close at 20?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. The 3 beta restaurants are live, and their measured results (bookings, no-shows, direct orders, commission avoided) replace the fictional figures on the site before paid selling starts.
2. Add a guarantee tied to an outcome that the CFO's unit economics confirm the business can afford.
3. Add a demand-transfer piece to the stack (for example, done-for-you material that moves platform customers to ordering direct) so the outcome does not depend on the owner's own effort.
4. Competitor research shows a difference that is more than price. If it does not, reposition around the outcome before scaling.
